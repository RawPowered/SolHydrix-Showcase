Surface and application requirements

The provisional documents identify flat and barrel concrete tile, metal roofing, and asphalt shingles, subject to compatibility verification. Clay tile requires separate confirmation.

Repair defects and flashing before application. Surfaces must be dry, clean, and sound. Verify adhesion and compatibility on a test area. Avoid conditions where rain, condensation, or unsuitable temperatures may affect curing.

Professional applicators must use appropriate PPE and fall protection. Refer to the product-specific Safety Data Sheet. Safety documentation, approvals, and warranties must be issued or confirmed by the manufacturer. Final product instructions govern application.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you
book an assessment.

Will SolHydrix work on my roof?

The provisional specifications identify flat and barrel concrete tile, metal roofing, and asphalt shingles. Your roof needs a condition assessment and compatibility test. Clay and other surfaces require separate confirmation.

Could coating help me postpone replacement?

An assessment can establish whether your roof is suitable for coating. The provisional SolHydrix documents do not establish how long replacement could be deferred or a savings amount. Roofs with structural problems or at the end of their service life may need replacement.

Will it change how my roof looks?

The specified finish is clear and semi-gloss. A test area helps you review the appearance and adhesion before the full application.

Can it fix a leaking roof?

Leaks, damaged flashing, and structural problems need appropriate roofing repairs before sealer application.

How long does application take?

The schedule depends on preparation, roof size, and weather. Provisional drying times are about 2 hours to touch dry and 4 hours to recoat at 77°F and 50% relative humidity. These are not full-cure times.

Does it extend roof life or include a warranty?

A measured increase in roof life has not been established by the provisional documents. Any warranty must be issued or confirmed by the manufacturer. Review written terms before approving treatment.
